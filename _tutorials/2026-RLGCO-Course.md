This tutorial was hosted at [LoG 2026](https://logconference.org) on November 21st, 2026, at 16:00 EST. It consisted of a 2.5-hour session introducing Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Graph Combinatorial Optimization (GCO).

Graph-based Combinatorial Optimization (GCO) problems involve finding discrete structures that optimize a given objective function and play a critical role in many real-world systems. Although traditional exact optimization methods can provide optimal solutions, they quickly become computationally prohibitive as graph sizes grow. This motivates the use of heuristic and approximation methods, which often require substantial problem-specific expertise.

Machine Learning (ML), and particularly Reinforcement Learning (RL), offers an alternative by enabling agents to automatically learn heuristics for constructing high-quality GCO solutions through reward-driven interactions. Such approaches can achieve competitive solution quality with fast inference while automating the complex trial-and-error process of heuristic design.

This tutorial aims to bridge the gap between Reinforcement Learning, Graph Learning, and Combinatorial Optimization. It (i) introduces GCO problems, traditional optimization methods, and modern ML approaches, (ii) presents the Markov Decision Process (MDP) formalism and demonstrates how to formulate GCO problems as MDPs, (iii) explores Deep Reinforcement Learning and graph representation learning methods and their integration into GCO pipelines, and (iv) discusses evaluation methodologies, scalability, generalization, and practical applications of RL for GCO.

The tutorial was delivered by **Franco Terranova** (Université de Lorraine, CNRS, INRIA, LORIA) and **Victor-Alexandru Darvariu** (University of Sheffield).
